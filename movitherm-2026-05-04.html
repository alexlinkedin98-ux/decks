ACCOUNT AUDIT SUMMARY

You don't have a budget problem.
You have a fragmentation problem.

Consolidate the campaigns. Fix the conversion signal. Then let smart bidding do what it's actually good at — in that order.

CORE DIAGNOSIS

The account is structured the way Google Ads worked 10–15 years ago — campaigns split by product and by match type (exact/phrase vs. broad). The same search terms are matching across multiple campaigns, fighting each other for budget. Fire Detection Camera shows up as a search term inside the broad campaign and the exact campaign at the same time.

Total volume looks healthy at first glance — 179 conversions across the last 90 days — but no single campaign clears the 30-conversions-in-30-days threshold that smart bidding actually needs to learn. So Google has been running on Manual CPC and Maximize Conversions, neither of which can optimize toward the leads that actually become customers.

Consolidate the structure and the conversion signal stops being noise. Smart bidding gets enough data to compound. The spend stops fragmenting and starts working.

01

WHAT BROKE — CONVERSION TRACKING IS LEAKING

One product conversion goal is showing zero recent conversions

The lead conversion is split out by product line, which makes sense in principle — different products, different value, different attribution. But one of the splits has stopped firing. Either the tag is broken, or the volume is so thin it's effectively dark, but it needs verification before any bidding decision relies on it.

FIX: Audit every conversion goal end-to-end before any structural changes. Confirm which are firing, which are stale, and which need to be merged or retired.

Conversion values are inconsistent across goals

One of the product-level conversions is reporting a slightly different value than the others. The moment we move toward Target ROAS or value-based bidding, that mismatch will skew the algorithm toward the wrong product line.

FIX: Normalize the conversion values. Either match them to the offer's economics or set them all to a unit value if the products aren't differentiable on margin.

Calls from ads have collapsed in the last 90 days

Looking at trends: call conversions from the ad units have dropped to near-zero recently. Website calls are reporting zero across the board. Phone is clearly part of the buyer journey — but right now we have no signal back into Google Ads when a phone conversion happens.

FIX: Repair the call extension tracking. Then deploy CallRail with dynamic number insertion on the site so every phone conversion is attributable back to a campaign and a keyword. That single change roughly doubles the conversion data Google can optimize against for a B2B account.

The contact form is the actual primary conversion

Stripping out the noise, the contact form is moviTHERM's biggest converting action — but the way the goals are split right now creates a real risk of double-counting between the per-product splits and the consolidated form fill.

FIX: Run the full tracking overhaul Grow My Ads' tracking team handles for every new client — confirm every goal, map double counting, set the right primary vs. secondary flags, and lock in the OCT integration with the CRM before any bid strategy changes.

02

WHAT BROKE — A 2010-ERA ACCOUNT STRUCTURE

Match-type segmentation is splitting your own budget against itself

The current setup has separate campaigns for exact/phrase match vs. broad match for each product line. That was a defensible strategy 10–15 years ago when Google's match-type targeting was rigid. With the way Google matches today, the same search queries land in both campaigns. They're competing with themselves for impression share, and the budget gets fragmented across small low-volume campaigns.

FIX: Consolidate every product line into a single campaign. Use product-level ad groups within consolidated campaigns instead of product-level campaigns. That preserves the per-product control you need (CPA targets, ad copy variation) while letting the algorithm see the full conversion signal.

Bidding is stuck below smart-bidding's threshold

Smart bidding (TCPA, TROAS, MaxConv with target) needs roughly 30 conversions per campaign per 30 days to learn. moviTHERM's account has 179 total conversions in 90 days — enough volume in aggregate, but split so thin across so many campaigns that no single one reaches the threshold. That's why several campaigns are still on Manual CPC.

FIX: After consolidation, move to Maximize Conversions with Target CPA on the consolidated search campaigns. As volume builds, layer Target ROAS where conversion values are clean. PMax should sit at a tighter CPA target than search so the smart-bidding waterfall layers correctly.

DSA is doing what Broad + AI Max already do

The standalone DSA campaign is, at this point, redundant. Broad match plus AI Max gives the same coverage with more control over URLs and headlines.

FIX: Retire DSA. Roll its query coverage into the consolidated broad-match groups inside the main campaigns.

Ad schedule is choking off learning

The current schedule is roughly business hours, Monday to Friday — clearly built around the assumption that the team picks up phones during the day. The problem: if the phone-call conversion path is broken (it is), and the form is the actual converting path, restricting the schedule means the algorithm never gets to learn about the off-hour visitors who would have converted on the form.

FIX: Open the ad schedule fully. If phone availability is the concern, configure the call extension to hide the phone number outside business hours rather than pausing the ad. The form stays available 24/7.

03

WHAT'S WORKING — DOUBLE DOWN HERE

Ad copy quality is genuinely strong

Ad strength across the active groups is rated Excellent and Good. That's not a given — most accounts at this spend level have a couple of half-finished groups dragging their average down. moviTHERM's ad copy is doing real work.

FIX: Carry the strongest performers into the consolidated campaigns. Then layer a second and third ad variant per group to get coverage across the three live ads Google allows — using the proven copy as the control.

Site links are configured the right way

Site link coverage is broad and managed at the account level rather than campaign level — which is the right call. Account-level assets are easier to maintain and they show up across whatever campaign is serving.

FIX: Use the site links to cross-promote products. A campaign focused on one product can carry site links pointing to the other product lines — that's how a B2B audience discovers adjacent capabilities they didn't know to search for.

04

ASSETS — UNDERPOPULATED

Site links are solid; everything else is lean. Callouts and structured snippets are the lowest-effort, highest-leverage piece of housekeeping in the account today.

  • Callouts — current set is thin. Each ad serving with full callout coverage gets meaningfully more SERP real estate.
  • Structured snippets — barely populated. Add models, services, and applications to surface in the SERP.
  • Cross-product site links — campaigns aren't currently advertising adjacent product lines. That's a free cross-sell layer.

FIX: Build out callouts and structured snippets at the account level. Add cross-product site links. None of this requires more spend — just configuration.

05

PERFORMANCE MAX — RESTRUCTURE FOR VOLUME

Single-product PMax can't get to smart bidding

The current PMax campaign is focused on one product line, which keeps the spend low and pins it to Maximize Conversions. PMax needs scale to work — splitting it down to one product is the same fragmentation pattern as the search side.

FIX: Restructure PMax with asset groups for every product line, run it as one consolidated campaign with the budget that all the product PMax campaigns currently share. Once volume hits the smart-bidding threshold, move to Target CPA with a target slightly tighter than the search campaigns.

Asset quality is good — that's not the bottleneck

Asset group quality scores are healthy. The headlines, descriptions, and images are all in the green. The bottleneck isn't asset quality — it's structure.

PMax should run a tighter CPA target than search. That's counterintuitive. The search campaigns should officially get priority on search terms, but the bidding waterfall works better when PMax aggressively bids on lower-funnel queries while search owns the high-intent exact matches.

LANDING PAGE — THE SECONDARY LEVERAGE

A PageSpeed test on the primary landing page surfaced two issues: load time is slow enough to affect Quality Score, and there are visible Cumulative Layout Shifts during render. Both directly hurt ad performance through Google's quality scoring, even before they touch conversion rate.

FIX: The site speed and CLS fixes are a development task on moviTHERM's side. Grow My Ads can spec the issues precisely (scripts blocking render, image dimensions, font loading), but the actual fixes require touching the site, which sits outside the ad-account scope.

EXPECTATION SETTING — THIS IS A 90-DAY REBUILD

MONTH 1

Tracking rebuild and structural consolidation. CPA may go up briefly while smart bidding learns the cleaner conversion signal.

MONTH 2

Smart bidding starts compounding once consolidated campaigns clear 30 conversions / 30 days. Schedule opens up; calls flow through CallRail.

MONTH 3

PMax restructure pays off. Cost per lead stabilizes downward. Layer remarketing across YouTube and LinkedIn on top of warm CRM lists.

AFTER

Push spend on the campaigns that hit target CPA / ROAS. OCT pipeline data starts feeding bids — Google bids harder for the leads that actually become customers.

What Grow My Ads doesn't promise: any improvement in the first 30 days. Smart bidding can't stabilize until the conversion signal is clean and the consolidated campaigns have enough volume to learn. Site-speed and layout-shift fixes are on moviTHERM's side — and they affect Quality Score regardless of how the ad account is structured.

Everything in this audit is execution and patience. The playbook is sound; the rest is the discipline to wait three months for the signal to compound.
